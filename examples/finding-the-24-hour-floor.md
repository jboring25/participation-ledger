# The 24-hour floor

The first real finding, and it did not come from comparing two cities. It came
from reading what the law actually requires.

## The claim under examination

Every jurisdiction that approves one of these projects can say the same
sentence: *the public had the opportunity to comment.* The study exists to ask
what that sentence is actually claiming.

It turns out to be answerable, precisely, because the opportunity has a
statutory floor and the floor is written down.

## What Arizona requires

**A.R.S. § 38-431.02** requires at least **24 hours** notice of a meeting, and
under subsection (G) the agenda must be available at least 24 hours before it.
The 24-hour period excludes Sundays and holidays.

That is the floor. One business day.

Marana's own agenda states the standard it is meeting, verbatim:

> This Notice and Agenda Posted no later than 24 hours prior to the meeting

## What the floor permits

On **January 6, 2026**, the Marana Town Council rezoned roughly **611 acres**
across two parcels for a hyperscale data center drawing up to **750 megawatts
at full build per site**.

From the town's own staff report for that item:

> This public hearing was noticed in The Daily Territorial newspaper, and all
> property owners within 300 feet of the rezoning area were noticed by United
> States Mail of the date, time, and topic of the public hearing.

**A 300-foot mailed-notice radius for a 600-acre facility.** Same report, on
the neighborhood meeting: *"there were approximately ten neighbors present."*

Nothing here was violated. Every requirement was met exactly. That is the
point.

## Set against Chandler

Chandler posted its agenda on **December 4, 2025** and voted on **December 11**.
Seven days, which is seven times the statutory floor and appears to be
voluntary.

In those seven days: 129 written submissions, 118 opposed, 41 speakers, and the
project died 7-0.

The submission curve is the part that matters:

| Day | Filed |
|---|--:|
| Dec 5 | 2 |
| Dec 6 | 1 |
| Dec 7 | 7 |
| Dec 8 | 6 |
| Dec 9 | 6 |
| Dec 10 | 26 |
| **Dec 11 (vote)** | **80** |

**62% of all participation arrived on the final day**, with seven days
available. The response was still accelerating when the vote happened.

## The finding

Put the two together and the sentence resolves.

**The legal standard for "opportunity to comment" is 24 hours. The observed
time for a public to actually assemble a response is longer than seven days,
because at day seven the curve had not yet turned over.**

The gap between those two numbers is not an accident, a failure of civic
virtue, or apathy. It is the designed distance between formal access and
effective access, and it is legal.

This is why the participation question is not psychological. A public that
produces 129 submissions in seven days, 80 of them on the last day, is not
apathetic. It is late, and it is late because the notice period is calibrated
to a floor that predates a world in which one zoning decision commits regional
water and power for decades.

## Why this matters for the argument

The philosophical claim was that responsibility transfers at the moment of
visibility, and that whoever maintains opacity holds it until then. That
claim previously rested on an intuition.

It now rests on a statute, two primary records, and a response curve.

Nobody hid anything. That is precisely the problem: a process can satisfy
every disclosure requirement it has and still make participation
functionally unavailable. The alibi is not a lie. It is a true sentence
doing work it has not earned.

## What still has to be checked

- The 611 acres is the sum of two parcels (300.5 and 310.7) rezoned in the
  same meeting. Under the frozen unit rule that is **one decision**.
- Marana's vote count is currently from press reporting, not the minutes.
  **Not citable until the minutes are pulled.**
- Marana's agenda posting date is not yet confirmed from the record, only the
  24-hour standard printed on the agenda itself. The actual posting date must
  be established before a notice window is computed for it.
- Whether Chandler's 7 days is required by Chandler code or is discretionary
  is unresolved and matters a great deal. If discretionary, the comparison is
  between two choices. If required, it is between two codes.
